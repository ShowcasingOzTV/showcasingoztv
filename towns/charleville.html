Charleville (QLD)

Charleville is a major regional centre in south-western Queensland, positioned beside the Warrego River and surrounded by the open landscapes of the Mulga Lands. The town provides commercial, health, education, transport and community services across an immense area of Outback Queensland.

The town developed during the nineteenth century as pastoral activity expanded across western Queensland. Sheep stations, cattle properties, wool production and regional trade became central to Charleville’s growth, while its position on important road, rail and stock routes strengthened its role as a service centre.

Charleville’s railway connection reinforced its importance as a transport hub and helped connect remote communities with eastern Queensland. Aviation later became another defining part of the town’s story, particularly through early Qantas operations, the Royal Flying Doctor Service and the establishment of a major United States Army Air Forces base during the Second World War.

Today, Charleville is known nationally for its combination of astronomy, wildlife conservation, aviation history and outback heritage. The clear inland skies support observatory and planetarium experiences, while the Charleville Bilby Experience promotes awareness of one of Australia’s most threatened native animals.

Historic buildings, museums and visitor experiences tell stories of pastoral settlement, communications, medicine, aviation and everyday life in remote Queensland. Hotel Corones remains one of the town’s most recognisable buildings and reflects the confidence and ambition that accompanied Charleville’s growth during the early twentieth century.

Charleville also provides a substantial range of accommodation, shops, dining and visitor services, making it an important base for travellers continuing towards Quilpie, Cunnamulla, Tambo, Augathella and other destinations across western Queensland.

📍 Attractions

Charleville’s attractions bring together astronomy, wildlife conservation, aviation, military history, pastoral heritage, local agriculture and the story of vital services developed to support remote Australia.

Explore attractions filming & feature options

  • Charleville Bilby Experience – Bilby Experience Tour Learn about the greater bilby, the threats facing the species and the conservation work supporting its survival. The guided experience gives visitors an opportunity to see bilbies in a purpose-built nocturnal environment while discovering their behaviour and ecological importance.
  • Charleville Cosmos Centre – Big Sky Observatory Explore Charleville’s famously clear night skies during a guided observatory experience. Powerful telescopes and knowledgeable guides introduce visitors to planets, star clusters, nebulae and other objects visible across the outback sky.
  • Charleville Cosmos Centre – Sun Viewing Our Star & Oasis in Space Planetarium Show Examine the Sun safely through specialised solar-viewing equipment before taking a planetarium journey exploring Earth’s place within the solar system and the conditions that make life possible.
  • Charleville Historic House & Museum Discover local history through collections of household items, photographs, machinery, transport exhibits and community records housed within one of Charleville’s significant historic buildings.
  • Hotel Corones Tour Step inside Charleville’s landmark 1929 hotel and learn about its architecture, aviation connections, famous guests and the remarkable story of founder Harry Corones.
  • Outback Date Farms Tour Visit a working family-operated date plantation and learn how dates are cultivated, harvested and prepared under the demanding conditions of south-western Queensland.
  • Outback Museum of Australia Explore the natural, social and cultural history of Outback Australia through a developing museum precinct near Charleville Airport, the Cosmos Centre and the town’s Second World War heritage attractions.
  • Royal Flying Doctor Service Charleville Visitor Centre Discover how aviation, medicine and radio communication were combined to provide health care across remote Australia. Displays explain the history of the Flying Doctor and the work undertaken from the Charleville base.
  • WWII Secret Base & Tour Learn about Charleville’s role as a strategically important United States Army Air Forces base between 1942 and 1946 through an interactive exhibition and guided tour of significant wartime locations.

This list is provided for visitor awareness only. Inclusion does not imply partnership or endorsement. Tours, sessions and experiences may require advance bookings and operate at scheduled times.

🗺️ Points of Interest & Landmarks

These landmarks reflect Charleville’s community identity and preserve the memory of significant events within the surrounding district.

Angellala Bridge Explosion Site Image Coming Soon

Angellala Bridge Explosion Site

Located approximately 30 kilometres southwest of Charleville, this memorial and interpretive site marks the location of the September 2014 road-train explosion that destroyed the former bridge. It also recognises the courage of the emergency personnel who responded to the incident.

Charleville Water Tower Art Image Coming Soon

Charleville Water Tower Art

Painted by artist Guido van Helten, this large monochromatic mural features children from the Charleville community. The water tower forms part of the Australian Silo Art Trail and has become one of the town’s most recognisable public artworks.
