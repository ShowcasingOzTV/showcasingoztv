Cameron Corner (QLD)

Cameron Corner is one of Australia’s most distinctive outback destinations, marking the remote point where Queensland, New South Wales and South Australia meet. Situated within the vast landscapes of Corner Country, the junction is surrounded by red sand, gibber plains, saltbush country and seemingly endless horizons.

The location takes its name from surveyor John Brewer Cameron, who completed the original survey of the Queensland and New South Wales border in 1880. The original wooden corner post was eventually replaced by the present survey marker, which allows visitors to stand at the meeting point of three Australian states.

Cameron Corner is not a conventional township. It is a remote landmark, traveller stop and service point positioned beside the Wild Dog Fence. The nearby Cameron Corner Store provides fuel, meals, refreshments and motel-style accommodation for travellers crossing this isolated part of inland Australia.

The surrounding region forms part of a wider outback journey connecting Cameron Corner with Sturt National Park, Fort Grey and Lake Pinaroo. Travellers encounter sand dunes, arid plains, important wetlands, pastoral history and evidence of the region’s enduring Aboriginal cultural connections.

Reaching Cameron Corner requires careful preparation. Roads through the region are remote and may be unsealed, while conditions can change following rain or extreme weather. Visitors should check current road conditions, carry sufficient water and fuel, and ensure their vehicle is suitable for the planned route.

📍 Attractions

Cameron Corner offers a small but memorable collection of outback experiences, supported by nearby walks and heritage sites within the western section of Sturt National Park.

Explore attractions filming & feature options

  • Cameron Corner Golf Course Play a memorable outback round on the informal nine-hole course associated with Cameron Corner, where the novelty comes from playing across three Australian states.
  • Cameron Corner Store The principal service point at the Corner provides fuel, meals, refreshments and accommodation, while serving as a meeting place for travellers, station workers and local residents.
  • Wells and Sturt’s Tree Walking Track Beginning near Fort Grey, this walking experience passes pastoral remains, old wells, river red gums and the shoreline of Lake Pinaroo. When conditions permit, walkers can continue across the dry lakebed to Sturt’s marked tree.

🗺️ Points of Interest & Landmarks

The landmarks around Cameron Corner reveal the area’s surveying history, pastoral heritage, conservation work and striking desert environment.

Cameron Corner Survey Marker

The marker identifies the point where Queensland, New South Wales and South Australia meet, making it one of Australia’s most recognisable geographical landmarks.

Fort Grey Historic Site

Fort Grey preserves connections with explorer Charles Sturt’s inland expedition and the pastoral history of the western section of Sturt National Park.

Greater Bilby Wire Sculpture Image Coming Soon

Greater Bilby Wire Sculpture

This large wire sculpture near Cameron Corner represents one of the native mammals being returned to the region through the Wild Deserts conservation program.

Lake Pinaroo Image Coming Soon

Lake Pinaroo

An internationally recognised ephemeral wetland approximately 24 kilometres southeast of Cameron Corner. When filled after rain, the lake provides important habitat for waterbirds and other wildlife.

Sturt National Park Image Coming Soon

Sturt National Park

This immense national park protects gibber plains, sand dunes, rocky ranges, ephemeral waterways and significant cultural and pastoral heritage across northwestern New South Wales.

Wild Dog Fence Image Coming Soon

Wild Dog Fence

The historic fence follows the state boundaries at Cameron Corner and forms part of one of the world’s longest fence systems. Travellers must close the border gate after passing through.
