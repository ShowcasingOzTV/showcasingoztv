Hay (NSW)

Hay is a historic regional town situated beside the Murrumbidgee River in the western Riverina of New South Wales. Surrounded by the immense Hay Plains, the town occupies an important position at the junction of the Sturt, Cobb and Mid-Western highways.

The Hay district forms part of the traditional country of Aboriginal peoples connected with the Murrumbidgee River and the surrounding plains. The river, wetlands and grasslands supported movement, food, cultural practices and community life long before the establishment of the present town.

European settlement expanded during the nineteenth century as pastoral properties were established across the surrounding plains. Hay developed near a significant Murrumbidgee River crossing and became an important service, transport and administrative centre for remote sheep stations and travelling stock routes.

Wool production and shearing became central to the district’s economy and identity. Large sheep stations employed generations of shearers, wool classers, cooks, working-dog handlers and transport operators. That history is interpreted at Shear Outback and remains visible throughout the wider district.

Hay also played an important role during the Second World War. Civilian internees and prisoners of war were held in camps near the town, including Jewish refugees transported to Australia aboard the HMT Dunera. Local museums preserve these complex stories and explain their continuing significance.

The Murrumbidgee River remains an important part of community life, supporting irrigation, recreation and the river red gum environment. Walking and cycling routes connect riverside reserves with public art, museums and other places throughout Hay.

📍 Attractions In And Around Hay

Hay’s museums preserve stories connected with pastoral work, wartime internment, education, community service and the development of this important Riverina town.

Bishop’s Lodge Historic House & Heritage Rose Garden

Bishop’s Lodge is an unusual iron residence built in 1888 for Anglican Bishop Sydney Linton. Its construction incorporated design features intended to reduce the effects of Hay’s intense summer heat. The restored house, outbuildings and heritage rose garden provide an insight into domestic life, architecture and religious history in the nineteenth-century Riverina.

Dunera Museum at Hay Railway Station

The Dunera Museum explores Hay’s role as an internment and prisoner-of-war centre during the Second World War. Displays at the historic railway station tell the stories of German, Italian and Japanese internees and prisoners, including the Jewish refugees remembered as the Dunera Boys. Photographs, documents and personal accounts connect events in Europe with the distant Hay Plains.

Hay Gaol Museum

Hay Gaol was constructed during the late nineteenth century and later served several different purposes, including use as a maternity hospital, wartime detention facility and institution for girls. The museum now preserves artefacts and stories associated with the building, the town and the wider district.

Hay War Memorial High School Museum

Hay War Memorial High School was created by the community as a practical memorial to local people who served during the First World War. The museum documents military service from the district as well as the history of the school, which opened on Anzac Day in 1923.

Shear Outback

Shear Outback and the Australian Shearers Hall of Fame celebrate the people, skills and traditions associated with Australia’s wool industry. Exhibitions, shearing demonstrations and stories of workers and working dogs explain how pastoral labour shaped Hay, the Riverina and rural Australia.

🗺️ Landmarks, Points of Interest & Hidden Gems

The Hay district combines riverside reserves, public art, historic roadside buildings, small communities and the uninterrupted horizons of the Hay Plains.

Bidgee Riverside Trail Image Coming Soon

Bidgee Riverside Trail

The Bidgee Riverside Trail follows the Murrumbidgee River through river red gum country between local reserves and Sandy Point. The formed trail can be explored on foot or by bicycle and features public sculptures that reflect the environment and history of the district.

Booligal Image Coming Soon

Booligal

Booligal is a small community north of Hay on the Cobb Highway, positioned beside the Lachlan River and surrounded by extensive pastoral country. The locality is closely associated with droving, wool production and Banjo Paterson’s poem Hay and Hell and Booligal.

Hay Water Tower Art Image Coming Soon

Hay Water Tower Art

Hay Water Tower Art is a large memorial mural painted by artist Matt Adnate across two water towers. It portrays five local Second World War veterans, including Indigenous soldier Private Victor George Murray and army nurse Lieutenant Lorna Margaret Whyte, representing the wider contribution made by people from the Hay district.

One Tree Hotel Image Coming Soon

One Tree Hotel

The historic One Tree Hotel stands beside the Cobb Highway between Hay and Booligal. Originally known as Finch’s Inn, it served travellers, drovers and workers moving across the plains. Although it no longer operates as a conventional hotel, the isolated building remains an important symbol of the district’s pastoral and transport history.

Sandy Point Beach Reserve Image Coming Soon

Sandy Point Beach Reserve

Sandy Point Beach Reserve occupies a bend of the Murrumbidgee River close to Hay. White sand, mature river red gums, lawns and picnic facilities make it a popular community recreation area. River conditions can change, so visitors should assess the water carefully and follow local safety advice.

Hay Plains Sunset Viewing Area Image Coming Soon

Sunset Viewing Area

The official Sunset Viewing Area is located north of Hay on the Cobb Highway. Its position on the exceptionally flat Hay Plains provides an uninterrupted 360-degree horizon, making it a memorable location for watching the sun set and observing the scale of the surrounding landscape.

Conditions along rural roads and beside the Murrumbidgee River can change because of heat, rain, flooding and maintenance. Confirm current access information before travelling beyond Hay.
