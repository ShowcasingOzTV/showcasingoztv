Port Fairy (VIC)

Port Fairy is a historic coastal town at the mouth of the Moyne River in south-west Victoria. Known for its working harbour, nineteenth-century architecture, sandy beaches and strong maritime character, it is one of the most distinctive seaside communities along Victoria’s western coast.

The district has been home to the Eastern Maar people for thousands of years. Its rivers, wetlands, coastline and offshore waters continue to hold deep cultural significance and form part of a landscape shaped by longstanding connections between people and Country.

European commercial activity began during the early nineteenth century when whalers and sealers operated along the coast. The settlement that developed around the Moyne River was initially known as Belfast before being renamed Port Fairy. Fishing, shipping, agriculture and maritime trade all contributed to the town’s early growth.

Prosperity generated through maritime and agricultural industries led to the construction of stone cottages, churches, hotels, warehouses and substantial public buildings. Many remain standing, giving Port Fairy one of regional Victoria’s most complete collections of nineteenth-century architecture.

The Moyne River and harbour remain central to the town. Commercial fishing vessels, recreational boats and historic structures line the waterfront, while nearby Griffiths Island protects important wildlife habitat and provides access to the Port Fairy Lighthouse and sweeping Southern Ocean views.

Port Fairy has also developed a strong reputation for arts, food, music and community events. Galleries, restaurants, cafés, boutiques and heritage accommodation complement the town’s beaches, coastal walks and historic streetscapes.

Its combination of working harbour, cultural heritage, dramatic coastal scenery and village atmosphere makes Port Fairy a valuable base for exploring south-west Victoria and the western end of the Great Ocean Road touring region.

🗺️ Points of Interest & Landmarks

Port Fairy’s landmarks reveal the town’s maritime history, preserved architecture and close relationship with the rugged coastline of south-west Victoria.

Battery Hill Image Coming Soon

Battery Hill

An elevated coastal site associated with Port Fairy’s nineteenth-century harbour defences. The reserve contains historic gun emplacements and provides views across the Moyne River entrance, harbour and coastline.

Codrington Wind Farm Image Coming Soon

Codrington Wind Farm

One of Australia’s earliest commercial wind farms, positioned in open coastal country west of Port Fairy. Its turbines demonstrate how the region’s powerful and consistent winds are used to generate renewable energy.

Griffiths Island & Port Fairy Lighthouse Image Coming Soon

Griffiths Island (including Port Fairy Lighthouse)

A coastal reserve at the mouth of the Moyne River, reached by a pedestrian causeway. A walking trail circles the island and passes the historic lighthouse, rocky shoreline, beaches and important seabird habitat.

Port Fairy Town Centre Image Coming Soon

Port Fairy Town Centre

A remarkably intact historic centre containing nineteenth-century cottages, churches, civic buildings and former commercial premises alongside contemporary shops, galleries, cafés and restaurants.

The Crags Image Coming Soon

The Crags

A dramatic coastal reserve west of Port Fairy featuring rugged cliffs, offshore rock formations and expansive Southern Ocean views. The area also has cultural significance for the Eastern Maar people.
