Casterton (VIC)

Casterton is a historic rural town in western Victoria, positioned beside the Glenelg River and surrounded by rolling farmland, river valleys and productive grazing country. Located within the Glenelg Shire, the town serves as an important commercial, agricultural and community centre for properties and smaller settlements across the surrounding district.

European settlement developed during the nineteenth century as pastoral activity expanded across western Victoria. Sheep grazing, wool production and cattle farming became central to the local economy, while the town’s location near the river helped support the growth of transport, trade and essential services.

Casterton is widely recognised as the birthplace of the Australian Kelpie. The breed traces its local history to a black-and-tan working dog named Kelpie, which became closely associated with Warrock Station north of the town. The Kelpie’s intelligence, endurance and livestock-working ability helped establish the breed as an enduring part of Australian pastoral life.

This working-dog heritage remains an important part of Casterton’s identity. It is celebrated through the Australian Kelpie Centre and the annual Australian Kelpie Muster, which attracts working dogs, handlers, breeders and visitors from across Australia.

The Glenelg River contributes strongly to Casterton’s landscape and recreational character. Riverfront reserves, walking areas and nearby Ess Lagoon provide places for fishing, picnics, exercise, birdwatching and quiet appreciation of the town’s natural setting.

Casterton’s town centre combines heritage buildings with shops, cafés, accommodation, community facilities and essential services. Nearby communities including Coleraine, Digby and Merino broaden the story of a district shaped by agriculture, transport, small businesses and generations of rural families.
