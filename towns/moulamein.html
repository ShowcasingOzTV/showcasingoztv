Moulamein, New South Wales

Riverina history, pastoral heritage and peaceful waterways at the meeting of the Edward River and Billabong Creek.

Discover Moulamein

Moulamein is a historic rural town in southern New South Wales, located at the meeting of the Edward River and Billabong Creek. Recognised as the oldest town in the Riverina, it offers visitors an opportunity to explore the early pastoral, river-trading and settlement history of the region in a peaceful country setting.

The waterways surrounding Moulamein have shaped the town’s development and identity. Long before European settlement, this landscape held deep cultural importance for the Wamba Wamba people, who used the rivers, creeks and surrounding country for hunting, fishing, camping and ceremonial gatherings.

During the nineteenth century, Moulamein developed as an inland port and service centre for the surrounding pastoral district. Paddle steamers transported wool, produce and supplies along the river system, linking remote farming properties with larger markets and river ports.

The Old Moulamein Wharf remains a visible connection to this riverboat era. Nearby historic buildings, including the former courthouse, help illustrate Moulamein’s early importance as an administrative and commercial centre for a widely dispersed population.

Pastoral history remains central to the local story. Mooloomoon Shearing Shed preserves the working history of the district’s sheep industry, while the Moulamein Heritage Village brings together historic buildings, machinery and artefacts associated with the region’s early settlers, tradespeople and farming families.

Natural attractions are equally important to the Moulamein experience. Moulamein Lake provides a tranquil location for walking, picnicking, birdwatching and family recreation, while the Edward River supports fishing and opportunities to enjoy the river red gum environment.

With its heritage attractions, riverfront landmarks, local accommodation and welcoming country character, Moulamein provides an interesting stop for travellers exploring the western Riverina and the wider River Country region.

🎟️ Attractions In And Around Moulamein

Mooloomoon Shearing Shed

Set among native bushland beside the Edward River, this historic shearing shed explores the lives of the shearers, settlers and pastoral families who helped shape Moulamein. Historical displays tell the story of the local wool industry and other significant Australian shearing sheds.

Moulamein Art Gallery

This welcoming local gallery showcases artwork, handcrafted items and gifts created by artists and makers from Moulamein and the surrounding region. It provides visitors with an opportunity to discover the creative side of the community while supporting local talent.

Moulamein Heritage Village

The Heritage Village preserves buildings, machinery, tools and household artefacts associated with the district’s pioneering history. Its collection includes structures such as an old schoolhouse and blacksmith’s shop, helping visitors understand everyday life in the developing Riverina.

Old Moulamein Courthouse

Dating from 1890, the former courthouse reflects Moulamein’s early role as an administrative and legal centre. Court services had operated in the district from the nineteenth century, serving residents across a large and sparsely populated portion of the Riverina.

Moulamein Lake

This peaceful lake covers approximately 15 hectares and provides an attractive setting for walking, picnicking, birdwatching and family recreation. Its calm water and surrounding vegetation create valuable habitat for waterbirds, including black swans.

Old Moulamein Wharf

Constructed in 1908, the wharf recalls the period when Moulamein operated as a busy inland river port. Paddle steamers once used the surrounding waterways to transport wool, agricultural produce, passengers and essential supplies throughout the region.

The Big Tree

This immense river red gum is one of the largest recorded examples in the Riverina, with a circumference of approximately 11.6 metres. Located along the Moulamein River Walk, it is an important natural landmark and meeting place within a culturally significant riverside landscape.
