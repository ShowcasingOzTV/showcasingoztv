Barkly Roadhouse (NT)

Barkly Roadhouse is a remote outback destination and essential highway stop situated on the Barkly Highway in the Northern Territory. The roadhouse is currently operated as Barkly Homestead and provides an important place for travellers to refuel, eat, rest and stay overnight while crossing the enormous Barkly Tableland.

Located between Tennant Creek in the Northern Territory and Camooweal near the Queensland border, Barkly Homestead stands on one of Australia’s great long-distance driving routes. Its isolated position makes it an important service centre for tourists, transport operators, pastoral workers and other travellers moving through this sparsely populated part of northern Australia.

The surrounding Barkly Tableland is characterised by extensive grasslands, working cattle stations, distant horizons and dramatic outback skies. The region’s immense scale becomes particularly apparent on the Barkly Highway, where travellers can journey for long distances between settlements and services.

Barkly Homestead provides fuel, meals, refreshments, visitor supplies and overnight accommodation in one convenient location. The facilities have been substantially rebuilt following the 2022 fire that destroyed the former roadhouse, allowing this important highway stop to continue serving the region and the travelling public.

Visitors should remember that this is an extremely remote part of Australia. Travellers should carry adequate drinking water, monitor fuel levels, check road and weather conditions, and ensure their vehicle is prepared for long-distance outback travel. Mobile telephone coverage may also be limited outside the immediate roadhouse area.

For many visitors, Barkly Roadhouse is more than a practical stop. It provides an opportunity to experience the isolation, hospitality and resilience that define life in the Australian outback, while breaking up one of the country’s longest and most memorable highway journeys.

Barkly Highway and Tablelands Highway Junction

This important outback junction connects the east–west Barkly Highway with the Tablelands Highway, which travels north through remote cattle country towards the Cape Crawford and Heartbreak Hotel area. The junction highlights Barkly Homestead’s strategic position within the Northern Territory road network.

Barkly Homestead Big Goannas

The large fighting-goanna sculpture at Barkly Homestead is one of the roadhouse’s most recognisable features. The distinctive outback artwork provides a popular photograph stop and reflects the wildlife, creativity and roadside character associated with remote Australian travel.

Barkly Tableland

The Barkly Tableland is an immense region of open grasslands, pastoral properties and expansive skies. Its flat horizons, changing colours and dramatic sunsets provide travellers with a powerful sense of the scale and isolation of Australia’s northern interior.

Frewena Rest Area

Frewena Rest Area is a public highway rest point located east of Barkly Homestead on the Barkly Highway. It provides motorists with a place to pause during the long drive across the tableland. Travellers should not rely on the rest area for fuel, accommodation or the full range of services available at Barkly Homestead.
