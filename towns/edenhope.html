Edenhope (VIC)

Edenhope is a rural town in western Victoria, positioned on the southern shore of Lake Wallace and approximately 30 kilometres from the South Australian border. It serves as an important community and service centre for the surrounding farms and smaller settlements of the West Wimmera.

The district forms part of the traditional country of the Jadawadjali people, whose cultural connections with the region’s lakes, wetlands, plants, wildlife and surrounding landscapes extend across generations. These waterways remain important environmental and cultural features of western Victoria.

European pastoral settlement developed during the nineteenth century, with sheep grazing and later grain production becoming central to the district’s economy. Edenhope grew as a supply, administrative and social centre for rural properties spread across the surrounding plains.

Lake Wallace is the town’s defining natural feature. When water levels allow, the lake supports fishing, boating and other recreational activities, while its shoreline provides walking, cycling, picnicking and birdwatching opportunities. A track follows the lake and connects several of Edenhope’s community facilities and landmarks.

Edenhope also holds an important place in Australian sporting history. The district is connected with members of the Aboriginal cricket team that toured England in 1868, the first organised Australian sporting team to compete internationally. A monument near Lake Wallace recognises this significant cultural and sporting story.

The town centre contains local shops, hospitality businesses, community services and heritage buildings. The former courthouse, information centre and Red Tail Gallery help visitors explore local history, art and the character of the wider West Wimmera district.

Nearby Goroke provides another perspective on regional life through its agricultural streetscape and large-scale silo artwork. Together, these communities demonstrate the resilience and creativity of towns located across Victoria’s western border country.

📍 Attractions

Edenhope’s attractions introduce visitors to the town’s heritage, community stories, visitor information and the creative work produced throughout the West Wimmera.

Goroke & Silo Art

Goroke is a small West Wimmera town surrounded by productive agricultural country. Its large-scale silo artwork celebrates regional wildlife and community identity while attracting visitors travelling through Victoria’s western farming districts.

Lake Wallace & Aboriginal Cricket Team Monument

Lake Wallace is Edenhope’s principal natural and recreational landmark, offering shoreline views, walking areas and important bird habitat. The nearby monument commemorates members of the Aboriginal cricket team that toured England in 1868 and recognises their place in Australian sporting history.
