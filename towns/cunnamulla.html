Cunnamulla (QLD)

Cunnamulla is an established outback town in south-west Queensland, located on the banks of the Warrego River. It serves as the administrative and commercial centre of the Paroo Shire and provides important services for surrounding pastoral properties, smaller communities and travellers moving through western Queensland.

The town developed during the late nineteenth century as pastoralism, transport and regional trade expanded across the district. Sheep and cattle grazing became central to the local economy, while stock routes, rail connections and road transport helped establish Cunnamulla as an important regional service centre.

Cunnamulla’s history is closely connected with the Warrego River. The river and its surrounding floodplain support native vegetation, birdlife, recreation and pastoral production. River walks, parkland and the Allan Tannock Weir allow visitors to experience this important part of the local landscape.

Artesian water has also played a major role in sustaining Cunnamulla and the wider region. Water drawn from the Great Artesian Basin has supported local communities, livestock and industry for generations. Today, the Cunnamulla Hot Springs presents that natural resource as a contemporary wellness and visitor experience beside the Warrego River.

The Cunnamulla Fella has become the town’s most recognisable symbol. Inspired by the Australian stockman celebrated in the song written by Stan Coster and recorded by Slim Dusty, the figure represents the humour, independence and resilience associated with life in the outback.

Cunnamulla also provides a base for exploring nearby Eulo and other destinations throughout the Paroo Shire. Eulo is known for its artesian mud springs, opal history, local characters and the story of the Eulo Queen, adding another distinctive chapter to the region’s visitor experience.

Heritage sites, public artworks, river environments, hot springs and locally operated businesses give visitors several ways to understand the town. Its hotels, motels and caravan parks support both touring travellers and people working across the surrounding pastoral district.

📍 Attractions

Cunnamulla and nearby Eulo offer visitor experiences connected with pastoral history, artesian water, local characters, opal heritage and the stories of life in Queensland’s south-west.

Explore attractions filming & feature options

  • Artesian Mud Baths Eulo A private bathing experience using mineral-rich artesian water and locally sourced mud. The attraction draws on Eulo’s connection with the Great Artesian Basin and offers visitors an unusual outback wellness experience.
  • Artesian Mud Springs Natural mud springs near Eulo created by groundwater moving through the surrounding landscape. The springs demonstrate the geological forces and underground water systems found throughout the Paroo Shire.
  • Cunnamulla All Aboard An immersive sound-and-light presentation inside the town’s former railway station. The experience uses local characters and stories to explore the role of rail transport in Cunnamulla’s history.
  • Cunnamulla Fella Visitor Information Centre The town’s main visitor-information facility, incorporating local history, regional displays, the Artesian Time Tunnel and information for exploring Cunnamulla and the wider Paroo Shire.
  • Cunnamulla Hot Springs A riverside bathing and wellness facility using naturally heated artesian water. Its pools provide different temperatures and views across the Warrego River environment.
  • Eulo Lizard Racing Track A distinctive reminder of Eulo’s community events and unconventional outback entertainment, including the lizard races historically associated with the township.
  • Eulo Queen Opal Centre A local opal and visitor attraction connected with Eulo’s opal history and the colourful story of Isabel Gray, remembered throughout the district as the Eulo Queen.

🗺️ Points of Interest & Landmarks

These landmarks, river locations and nearby communities contribute to Cunnamulla’s identity and help tell the broader story of settlement, water, transport and community life in the Paroo Shire.

Allan Tannock Weir Image Coming Soon

Allan Tannock Weir

Located on the Warrego River south of town, the weir supports local water management and provides a scenic recreation area with river access, picnic facilities and opportunities for fishing and birdwatching.

Cunnamulla Fella Image Coming Soon

Cunnamulla Fella

This prominent statue celebrates the Australian stockman portrayed in the song written by Stan Coster and recorded by Slim Dusty. It has become Cunnamulla’s most recognisable landmark and a symbol of the town’s pastoral identity.

Cunnamulla Town Sign Image Coming Soon

Cunnamulla Town Sign

The town sign marks arrival in Cunnamulla and provides a popular photograph for travellers entering this well-known Warrego River community in south-west Queensland.

Cunnamulla Water Tower Image Coming Soon

Cunnamulla Water Tower

A prominent feature of the local skyline representing the importance of reliable water infrastructure to residents, businesses and industries operating in an arid inland environment.

Rotunda Bob Poncho Park Image Coming Soon

Rotunda Bob Poncho Park

A central community park and gathering space providing shaded areas, visitor facilities and a convenient place to pause while exploring the Cunnamulla town centre.

The Robber’s Tree Image Coming Soon

The Robber’s Tree

A historic site associated with the story of Joseph Wells, who robbed the local bank in 1880 before attempting to hide near the tree. The location preserves one of Cunnamulla’s best-known early settlement stories.

Wyandra Image Coming Soon

Wyandra

A small outback settlement north of Cunnamulla with links to pastoralism, railway history and the long-distance transport routes connecting communities throughout western Queensland.

Yumba Reserve Image Coming Soon

Yumba Reserve

A culturally and historically important local area associated with Cunnamulla’s Aboriginal community. Its story forms part of the town’s broader social history and continuing connection to Country.
