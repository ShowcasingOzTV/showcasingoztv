Halls Creek (WA)

Halls Creek is a remote Kimberley town situated on the Great Northern Highway in northern Western Australia. Surrounded by red earth, spinifex-covered ranges and immense open landscapes, the town provides an important service centre for residents, surrounding Aboriginal communities, pastoral properties and travellers crossing the eastern Kimberley.

The district has an important place in Western Australian history. Gold was discovered near the original Halls Creek settlement in 1885, helping to trigger Western Australia’s first major gold rush. Although the rush was relatively short-lived, it contributed to the development of transport, trade and settlement across the Kimberley.

The present-day town was developed away from the original goldfield settlement and now supports travellers using the Great Northern Highway, Duncan Road and the Tanami Road. Fuel, supplies, accommodation, visitor information and essential services make Halls Creek a significant stopping point between Fitzroy Crossing and Kununurra.

Halls Creek is also a gateway to a remarkable collection of natural landmarks. White quartz formations, seasonal waterholes, rocky gorges and permanent freshwater springs reveal the geological diversity of the surrounding country. More distant journeys lead towards the Bungle Bungle Range and Kandimalal, commonly known as Wolfe Creek Crater.

Aboriginal culture is central to the identity of Halls Creek and the wider region. Local artists, families and Traditional Owners maintain deep cultural connections with Country, waterways, stories and landscapes. Places throughout the district often have both Aboriginal names and later European names, reflecting different layers of cultural and historical significance.

Travellers should remember that distances in the Kimberley are substantial and conditions can change quickly. Some surrounding roads are unsealed, access may be affected during the wet season and remote journeys require careful preparation. Visitors should check current road, weather and access information before leaving town.

Yarliyil Art Centre

Yarliyil Art Centre is an Aboriginal-owned arts organisation in Halls Creek that provides a place for local artists to create, exhibit and sell their work through an ethical community-based enterprise. The centre represents artists from Halls Creek and surrounding communities, with artworks expressing connections to Country, family, culture, history and the distinctive landscapes of the Kimberley. A visit offers travellers an opportunity to engage with locally produced Aboriginal art while directly supporting participating artists and the continuation of cultural expression.

🗺️ Landmarks, Points of Interest & Hidden Gems

The country surrounding Halls Creek contains geological formations, freshwater places and historic locations connected with the Kimberley’s Aboriginal heritage, gold-rush history and remote landscapes.

Caroline Pool Image Coming Soon

Caroline Pool

Caroline Pool, known as Wimirri in the Jaru language, is a natural waterhole surrounded by shady trees, broad sandy creek banks and rocky Kimberley country. The site was historically an important water source and became a popular gathering place for local families. Water levels vary considerably with seasonal rainfall, and access is by an unsealed road that may become corrugated or temporarily inaccessible.

China Wall Image Coming Soon

China Wall

China Wall is a striking natural formation created by a white quartz vein rising from the surrounding landscape. Its narrow, wall-like ridge extends across the red Kimberley terrain and creates a dramatic contrast against the surrounding bushland. Located a short drive from Halls Creek, it is one of the area’s most accessible geological landmarks and a popular photography stop.

Old Halls Creek Image Coming Soon

Old Halls Creek

Old Halls Creek marks the site of the original township that developed after the discovery of gold in 1885. The settlement once supported prospectors and residents with stores, government services, a hospital, police station and hotel. Surviving remains, memorials and nearby historic places provide insight into the hardships and ambitions of the Kimberley gold-rush period. Access is via Duncan Road and includes unsealed sections.

Palm Springs Image Coming Soon

Palm Springs

Palm Springs, also known by the Jaru name Lugangarna, is a spring-fed permanent freshwater pool on the Black Elvire River. Palm trees, rocky banks and surrounding vegetation give the location an oasis-like character that contrasts strongly with the wider dry landscape. It is reached by travelling along Duncan Road, and visitors should confirm road and water conditions before setting out.

Kandimalal / Wolfe Creek Crater Image Coming Soon

Wolfe Creek Meteorite Crater

Kandimalal, widely known as Wolfe Creek Meteorite Crater, is an immense meteorite impact structure located southeast of Halls Creek near the Tanami Road. The crater has major geological importance and profound cultural significance for Aboriginal people connected with the area. Reaching the national park involves remote travel, and a four-wheel-drive vehicle is strongly recommended. Road conditions, fuel requirements and park access should be checked before departure.

Conditions at natural and remote locations can change because of rainfall, flooding, heat, road closures and cultural access requirements. Check with the Halls Creek Visitor Information Centre and relevant authorities before travelling.
