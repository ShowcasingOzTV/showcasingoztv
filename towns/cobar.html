Cobar (NSW)

Cobar is an historic mining town in western New South Wales, positioned at the crossroads of the Barrier Highway and the Kidman Way. Surrounded by red-earth plains, mulga country and working pastoral properties, the town is an important service centre for travellers, local communities and major mining operations across the region.

The town’s modern story grew from the discovery of copper in the 1870s. Mining rapidly transformed the isolated site into a busy settlement, and Cobar later became associated with gold, silver, lead and zinc as the district’s mineral industry expanded. Mining remains central to the local economy and identity, while the town’s preserved buildings, machinery and memorials reveal the experiences of generations who worked above and below ground.

Visitors can explore that heritage through the Great Cobar Museum and Visitor Information Centre, the Cobar Miners Memorial and the open-air displays of Cobar Miners Heritage Park. The distinctive Cobar Sound Chapel adds a contemporary cultural dimension, combining music, art and the dramatic acoustics of a former water tank overlooking the outback landscape.

Beyond the town centre, Fort Bourke Hill Lookout provides a broad perspective across Cobar and the surrounding mining country. Farther afield, Mount Grenfell Historic Site protects an Aboriginal cultural landscape of profound significance, including rock-art galleries and walking routes through rugged ranges and open woodland.

Cobar also reflects the character of an enduring outback community. Its hotels, motels, caravan park, sporting clubs, local businesses and public spaces support residents and visitors travelling long distances across inland New South Wales. This combination of industry, heritage, culture and community gives the town a character that is both practical and unmistakably local.

Cobar Miners Heritage Park

Located near the Great Cobar Museum, this open-air heritage area displays mining machinery and interpretive material that help explain the equipment, working conditions and industries that shaped Cobar’s development.

Fort Bourke Hill Lookout

Fort Bourke Hill Lookout offers elevated views across Cobar, the surrounding outback and the scale of the nearby mining landscape. It is a useful vantage point for understanding how closely the town’s history and present-day economy are connected to mining.

Mount Grenfell Historic Site

Mount Grenfell Historic Site protects a culturally significant Aboriginal landscape west of Cobar. Rock-art galleries, walking tracks and the surrounding ranges provide visitors with insight into the enduring connection between the Ngiyampaa people and Country.
