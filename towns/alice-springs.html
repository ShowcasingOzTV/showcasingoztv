Alice Springs (NT)

Alice Springs, known as Mparntwe to its Traditional Owners, is the principal service centre of Central Australia and one of the country’s most distinctive inland destinations. The town sits between the East and West MacDonnell Ranges, where the usually dry Todd River passes through a landscape of red rock, spinifex, river red gums and wide desert skies.

The area is part of the traditional lands of the Central Arrernte people, whose continuing cultural connections, stories and knowledge are fundamental to the identity of Alice Springs. Aboriginal art, language and culture remain central to the town, reflected in its galleries, cultural organisations, public places and the communities of the wider region.

European settlement developed around the Overland Telegraph Station established in the early 1870s. The telegraph line connected Adelaide with Darwin and the wider world, while the later arrival of the railway strengthened the town’s role as a transport, government and commercial hub. Historic buildings, museums and preserved transport collections now interpret these important chapters of inland Australian history.

Modern Alice Springs supports communities across an enormous area of the Northern Territory. It is a centre for health, education, aviation, pastoral services, Aboriginal organisations, arts and tourism, and a base for exploring the MacDonnell Ranges, Larapinta Trail, desert waterholes, historic reserves and remote settlements of the Red Centre.

Visitors can experience desert wildlife, aviation and medical history, Aboriginal art, astronomy, walking trails and panoramic lookouts within and around the town. 📍 Attractions

Alice Springs offers an exceptional concentration of cultural, historical, wildlife and desert experiences that introduce visitors to the stories and environments of Central Australia.

Explore attractions filming & feature options

  • Adelaide House Museum A heritage building designed by Reverend John Flynn that interprets early health care, communication and community life in Central Australia.
  • Alice Springs Desert Park (Day & Nocturnal Tour) A major desert wildlife and cultural attraction presenting the plants, animals and living landscapes of Central Australia through habitat exhibits and guided experiences.
  • Alice Springs Reptile Centre A specialist wildlife attraction featuring Central Australian reptiles, including snakes, lizards and crocodiles, with informative presentations and supervised encounters.
  • Alice Springs School of the Air A visitor experience explaining how distance education connects teachers with students living across the vast and isolated communities of Central Australia.
  • Alice Springs Telegraph Station Historical Reserve The site of the original overland telegraph station and early township, with restored buildings, interpretation, walking trails and important local history.
  • Araluen Cultural Precinct A cultural hub bringing together galleries, museums, performing arts and collections that explore the art, history, geology and communities of Central Australia.
  • Boomerang Art – Aboriginal Art Gallery A local gallery presenting Aboriginal artworks and offering visitors an opportunity to learn more about Central Australian artistic traditions and contemporary practice.
  • Central Australian Aviation Museum A museum preserving aircraft, photographs and memorabilia connected with the pioneers and services that shaped aviation across Australia’s remote interior.
  • Earth Sanctuary Astronomy Dinner & Star Tour A guided evening experience combining dark-sky astronomy, desert surroundings and storytelling beneath Central Australia’s expansive night sky.
  • Megafauna Central An interpretive exhibition exploring the giant prehistoric animals once found in Central Australia and the significant fossil discoveries made at nearby Alcoota.
  • Museum of Central Australia A natural-history collection within the Araluen Cultural Precinct documenting the region’s geology, fossils, biodiversity and distinctive desert environments.
  • National Road Transport Hall of Fame & Old Ghan Train Museum A large transport heritage collection celebrating the road trains, commercial vehicles, machinery and railway history that helped connect remote Australia.
  • Royal Flying Doctor Service Museum An interactive visitor centre sharing the history and continuing importance of aeromedical care and communications across rural and remote Australia.
  • The Kangaroo Sanctuary (Outside Only) A wildlife sanctuary dedicated to rescuing and rehabilitating orphaned kangaroos. Access and tour arrangements should be confirmed directly before visiting.
  • This Is Aboriginal Art Centre A locally based art centre and gallery showcasing Aboriginal artworks while helping visitors engage with the artists, stories and cultural context behind the work.
  • Yubu Napa Art Gallery A contemporary gallery displaying Aboriginal art, sculpture and gifts, with a focus on work connected to Central Australia and the wider desert region.
