Kings Canyon (NT)

Kings Canyon is one of the defining natural landmarks of Australia’s Red Centre. Located within Watarrka National Park at the western end of the George Gill Range, the canyon is known for its towering sandstone walls, sheltered creek environment and sweeping views across the surrounding desert landscape.

Watarrka National Park has deep cultural importance for the Luritja and Arrernte peoples, whose connections with the landscape extend across many thousands of years. The canyon, waterholes, plants and geological features form part of a living cultural landscape containing places and stories that must be approached respectfully.

The canyon’s cliffs expose layers of sandstone shaped by erosion over hundreds of millions of years. Weathering has created sheer walls, sheltered gorges, domed formations and narrow passages that contrast strongly with the open plains and desert vegetation beyond the canyon.

Walking is the principal way to experience Kings Canyon. The demanding Rim Walk climbs above the canyon and follows its upper edges, while the easier Kings Creek Walk travels along the canyon floor. Heat and exposure can make conditions dangerous, particularly during the warmer months.

The Garden of Eden is a sheltered rockhole surrounded by plants that benefit from the canyon’s protected environment. It is also a culturally sensitive place. Visitors may use the designated walking route but must not swim in the waterhole or enter closed areas.

Kings Canyon is extremely remote, with limited fuel, accommodation and visitor services. Travellers need to carry sufficient water, monitor road and weather conditions and understand that mobile phone coverage may be unreliable across much of the surrounding region.

📍 Attractions

The principal visitor experiences at Kings Canyon are its established walking routes. Conditions can be demanding, so visitors should check temperatures, park alerts and track requirements before starting.

Explore attractions filming & feature options

  • Kings Canyon Rim Walk A challenging six-kilometre circuit that climbs steeply to the top of the canyon before following its sandstone rim. The route passes dramatic cliff edges, weathered rock formations and the Garden of Eden while providing extensive views across Watarrka National Park. The walk generally takes three to four hours. On days forecast to reach 36°C or higher, walkers must begin before 9:00 am.
  • Kings Creek Walk A shorter and less demanding return walk following Kings Creek along the canyon floor. The route provides views toward the towering canyon walls and introduces visitors to the sheltered vegetation growing within the gorge without requiring the steep climb of the Rim Walk.

This list is provided for visitor awareness only. Inclusion does not imply partnership or endorsement. Track access, closures and heat restrictions may change. Check official Watarrka National Park information before beginning any walk.

Garden of Eden

A sheltered rockhole and pocket of vegetation reached from the Kings Canyon Rim Walk. The waterhole is culturally significant and swimming is prohibited. Visitors must remain on the designated route and respect all signs and closures.

Kings Canyon

A vast sandstone canyon within Watarrka National Park, distinguished by sheer walls, sheltered gullies and elevated views across the George Gill Range and surrounding Central Australian landscape.

Kings Creek

The creek running through the base of Kings Canyon supports a protected corridor of vegetation and provides a striking contrast with the exposed sandstone walls and arid country surrounding the gorge.
