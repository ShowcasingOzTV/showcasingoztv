Louth (NSW)

Louth is a small and character-filled outback community situated beside the Darling River in north-western New South Wales. Located between Bourke and Tilpa, the village is surrounded by expansive grazing country, river landscapes and the unmistakable horizons of the Australian outback.

The town was established during the nineteenth century when the Darling River was an important transport and trade route. Louth developed as a service point for nearby pastoral stations, river travellers and the workers who supported the surrounding grazing industry.

Although the population is now small, Louth retains a strong community identity. Its historic buildings, riverside setting and connections with surrounding stations preserve the atmosphere of an authentic outback settlement rather than a conventional tourism centre.

Louth is widely known for the annual Louth Races, which bring visitors from across Australia to the village each August. The event transforms the normally quiet community into a major outback gathering while maintaining the relaxed hospitality for which the town is known.

The nearby Louth Cemetery contains one of the town’s most significant stories. The memorial to Mary Mathews was designed so the setting sun would reflect from its cross towards the former Mathews family home, creating an enduring connection between the town’s history and the surrounding landscape.

North of Louth, Toorale National Park and State Conservation Area protect extensive floodplains and the meeting country of the Darling and Warrego river systems. Travellers can explore scenic driving routes, pastoral heritage, riverside picnic areas and elevated viewpoints across this remote environment.

With limited services and considerable distances between communities, visitors should plan carefully, confirm road and weather conditions and carry suitable supplies. Those who make the journey will discover a welcoming riverside village and an especially memorable part of Outback New South Wales.

Louth Cemetery

The historic Louth Cemetery contains the memorial of Mary Mathews, commissioned by town founder Thomas Andrew Mathews following her death in 1886. Its polished cross was positioned to reflect the setting sun towards the family’s former home, creating one of Louth’s most distinctive historic stories.

Louth Town Centre Image Coming Soon

Louth Town Centre

Louth’s compact town centre sits beside the Darling River and preserves the quiet character of a remote pastoral settlement. Historic buildings, community facilities and the river crossing illustrate the village’s long-standing role as a meeting and service point for the surrounding district.

Many Big Rocks Picnic Area Image Coming Soon

Many Big Rocks Picnic Area – Karnu Yalpa

Many Big Rocks, known as Karnu Yalpa, is a remote riverside picnic and fishing area in Toorale National Park. Located along the Darling River driving route, it offers shade beneath river red gums and coolibahs, opportunities for birdwatching and views across the river’s natural rock formations.

The surrounding route incorporates the Darling River Drive and access through the wider Toorale landscape, including the Homestead Dam area. Conditions can be remote and changeable, so visitors should check current park alerts and road access before travelling.

Mount Talowla Lookout Image Coming Soon

Mount Talowla Lookout

Mount Talowla Lookout provides sweeping views across Toorale National Park and State Conservation Area. Known to the Kurnu-Baakandji People as Withawitha Iaana, the elevated viewpoint reveals the scale of the surrounding floodplains, river country and distant outback ranges.

Toorale Station Homestead Image Coming Soon

Toorale Station Homestead

The Toorale Station Homestead precinct preserves an important part of the region’s pastoral history. Established on a vast former grazing property, the precinct includes historic structures and interpretation connected with station life, river commerce and the development of the surrounding outback district.
