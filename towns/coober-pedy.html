Coober Pedy (SA)

Coober Pedy is one of Australia’s most distinctive outback towns, located in northern South Australia along the Stuart Highway. Internationally recognised as an important opal-mining centre, the town is equally famous for its underground homes, businesses, churches and accommodation carved into the surrounding sandstone.

Opal was discovered in the district in 1915, attracting prospectors and miners from across Australia and overseas. The difficult working conditions, isolation and extreme temperatures demanded resilience and ingenuity. Over time, these challenges helped create the multicultural mining community and unconventional underground lifestyle for which Coober Pedy is now known.

Many residents live in underground homes known as dugouts. These structures use the surrounding earth to maintain a more stable internal temperature throughout the year, providing relief from the intense summer heat. Visitors can experience this way of life through underground motels, museums, churches, galleries and former mines located throughout the town.

Coober Pedy’s opal heritage remains central to its identity. Working and historic mines, opal shops, museums and interpretive attractions explain how opal is located, extracted, processed and valued. The landscape surrounding the town is marked by mine shafts and mullock heaps, creating a striking visual record of more than a century of prospecting.

Beyond the town, the Kanku-Breakaways Conservation Park contains colourful mesas, escarpments and expansive desert plains. Nearby sections of the Dog Fence and Moon Plain further demonstrate the scale and isolation of the region. The unusual terrain has also attracted numerous film and television productions, contributing another layer to Coober Pedy’s international profile.

As a major service centre on the Stuart Highway, Coober Pedy provides accommodation, fuel, food, vehicle services and visitor facilities for travellers moving between South Australia and the Northern Territory. Its remote position makes it an important stop during one of Australia’s most memorable inland road journeys.

Dog Fence
