Coober Pedy (SA)

Coober Pedy is one of Australia’s most distinctive outback towns, located in northern South Australia along the Stuart Highway. Internationally recognised as an important opal-mining centre, the town is equally famous for its underground homes, businesses, churches and accommodation carved into the surrounding sandstone.

Opal was discovered in the district in 1915, attracting prospectors and miners from across Australia and overseas. The difficult working conditions, isolation and extreme temperatures demanded resilience and ingenuity. Over time, these challenges helped create the multicultural mining community and unconventional underground lifestyle for which Coober Pedy is now known.

Many residents live in underground homes known as dugouts. These structures use the surrounding earth to maintain a more stable internal temperature throughout the year, providing relief from the intense summer heat. Visitors can experience this way of life through underground motels, museums, churches, galleries and former mines.

Coober Pedy’s opal heritage remains central to its identity. Working and historic mines, opal shops, museums and interpretive attractions explain how opal is located, extracted, processed and valued. The surrounding landscape is marked by mine shafts and mullock heaps, creating a striking visual record of more than a century of prospecting.

Beyond the town, Kanku-Breakaways Conservation Park contains colourful mesas, escarpments and expansive desert plains. Nearby sections of the Dog Fence and Moon Plain further demonstrate the scale and isolation of the region.

The unusual terrain has also attracted film and television productions requiring otherworldly settings. Surviving props and local stories connected with these productions contribute another layer to Coober Pedy’s international profile.

As a major service centre on the Stuart Highway, Coober Pedy provides accommodation, fuel, food, vehicle services and visitor facilities for travellers moving between South Australia and the Northern Territory. Its remote position makes it an important stop during one of Australia’s most memorable inland road journeys.

Its combination of industry, cultural diversity, unconventional architecture and outback resilience makes it one of Australia’s most remarkable regional destinations.

📍 Attractions

Coober Pedy offers an unusual collection of underground homes, historic mines, churches, galleries, wildlife experiences and outback businesses that reveal the town’s mining heritage and distinctive way of life.

Explore attractions filming & feature options

  • Big Winch 360 A prominent hilltop attraction combining panoramic views across Coober Pedy with visitor facilities, local history, art and an immersive cinema experience.
  • Cadney Homestead Roadhouse A remote Stuart Highway roadhouse south of Coober Pedy providing fuel, meals, accommodation and essential services for outback travellers.
  • Catacomb Church An underground church carved into the sandstone and designed around the natural shape and texture of the excavated rock.
  • Crocodile Harry’s Underground Nest & Dugout The former underground home of Crocodile Harry, featuring carved walls, personal objects and artwork connected with one of Coober Pedy’s best-known characters.
  • Faye’s Underground Home A hand-excavated underground residence that demonstrates how local people adapted their homes to the climate and geology of Coober Pedy.
  • Josephine’s Gallery and Kangaroo Orphanage A local gallery and wildlife facility combining Aboriginal art, regional artwork and the care of rescued kangaroos.
  • Old Coober Pedy Jail A small historic building associated with local law enforcement and the challenges of maintaining services in a remote mining town.
  • Old Timers Mine An original opal mine with underground passages, mining displays, historic dugouts and exhibits explaining early life in Coober Pedy.
  • Serbian Orthodox Church An impressive underground church featuring carved sandstone walls, religious artwork and a spacious interior excavated beneath the desert surface.
  • The Big Miner A large miner sculpture celebrating the prospectors and workers who established Coober Pedy’s international reputation as an opal centre.
  • The Mail Run An outback touring experience following remote mail-delivery routes and connecting travellers with isolated stations, settlements and landscapes beyond Coober Pedy.
  • The Opal Bug A local opal business and distinctive roadside stop reflecting the creativity, humour and opal-trading culture found throughout the town.
  • Tom’s Opal Mine A former working mine where visitors can learn about opal geology, mining methods and the challenges of searching for opal underground.
  • Umoona Opal Mine & Museum An underground museum and opal attraction presenting Aboriginal culture, regional geology, mining history and the formation of precious opal.
