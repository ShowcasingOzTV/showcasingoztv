Brewarrina (NSW)

Brewarrina is an outback town situated on the banks of the Barwon River in north-western New South Wales. Located approximately 96 kilometres east of Bourke, the town serves as an important centre for surrounding communities, pastoral properties and travellers following the Kamilaroi Highway and Darling River Run.

Known locally as “Bre”, Brewarrina is a place of exceptional Aboriginal cultural significance. The town and surrounding river country are connected with the Ngemba, Murrawarri, Euahlayi, Weilwan, Ualarai and Baranbinja peoples, whose cultural, spiritual and political connections with Country have continued across thousands of generations.

At the heart of Brewarrina is Baiame’s Ngunnhu, the National Heritage-listed Aboriginal fish traps. The extensive system of stone weirs and holding ponds stretches along the Barwon River and demonstrates sophisticated knowledge of river flows, fish behaviour, engineering and sustainable food gathering.

The fish traps also served as an important intertribal meeting place. Aboriginal nations travelled to the area for ceremonies, trade, cultural exchange and the seasonal harvesting of fish. The site remains a living cultural place rather than simply an archaeological feature.

Brewarrina later developed as a river port and service centre during the expansion of pastoral activity and European settlement. Riverboats, historic buildings, the Barwon Bridge and the town’s museums preserve parts of this more recent history.

Visitors can explore the Brewarrina Aboriginal Cultural Museum, join a guided tour of Baiame’s Ngunnhu, follow the River Walk, visit the sacred Ochre Beds and learn about both Aboriginal and settler history throughout the town.

Brewarrina also offers fishing, birdwatching and quiet riverside environments. Travellers heading beyond town should prepare for long distances, changing road conditions, limited services and high temperatures during warmer months.

📍 Attractions

Brewarrina’s attractions share the town’s living Aboriginal culture, riverside environment, community history, local creativity and enduring connection to Country.

Explore attractions filming & feature options

  • Brewarrina Aboriginal Cultural Museum A culturally significant museum beside Baiame’s Ngunnhu, presenting immersive stories, local artwork and cultural knowledge connected with the Aboriginal peoples of the Brewarrina region. Guided tours of the museum and fish traps are also available.
  • Brewarrina Settlers Museum A local historical collection housed in a nineteenth-century building constructed from bricks made in Brewarrina. Its displays preserve photographs, household objects, machinery and other material associated with the town’s pastoral and settler history. Opening access may depend on volunteer availability.
  • Brewarrina Visitor Information and Exhibition Centre The town’s visitor information centre provides maps, brochures, local advice, regional itineraries, gifts and information about Brewarrina’s cultural and natural attractions.
  • Muddy Waters Coffee Shop and Gallery A locally operated café and gallery in Brewarrina’s main street, offering meals and refreshments alongside gifts, artwork and locally produced items.

🗺️ Points of Interest & Landmarks

Explore Brewarrina’s culturally significant river sites, historic structures and places of remembrance, which together reveal the depth and complexity of the town’s story.

Baiame’s Ngunnhu Image Coming Soon

Baiame’s Ngunnhu

The National Heritage-listed Brewarrina Aboriginal fish traps form an extensive network of stone weirs and ponds in the Barwon River. The site remains a place of profound cultural, spiritual and social significance for Aboriginal communities.

Barwon Bridge Image Coming Soon

Barwon Bridge

A historic lift-span bridge across the Barwon River that reflects Brewarrina’s former role as an inland river port. The movable span allowed riverboats to pass when commercial river traffic operated through the district.

Barwon River Image Coming Soon

Barwon River

The river is central to Brewarrina’s culture, environment and history. It supports wildlife, fishing and recreation while holding enduring significance for the Aboriginal peoples connected with this Country.

Brewarrina Mission Image Coming Soon

Brewarrina Mission

A State Heritage-listed place associated with the forced relocation and government control of Aboriginal people. The site carries difficult but important histories and should be visited with cultural sensitivity and respect.

Four Mile Reserve Image Coming Soon

Four Mile Reserve

A peaceful riverside area near Brewarrina offering opportunities for picnicking, fishing, birdwatching and appreciating the natural environment along the Barwon River.

Hospital Creek Memorial Image Coming Soon

Hospital Creek Memorial

Located approximately 17 kilometres north of Brewarrina, this memorial honours Aboriginal men, women and children killed during the Hospital Creek massacre. It is a place for quiet reflection, truth-telling and remembrance.

Ochre Beds Image Coming Soon

Ochre Beds

These State Heritage-listed ochre deposits lie downstream from Baiame’s Ngunnhu. Ochre from this sacred place has been used for ceremony, body decoration, storytelling and cultural exchange for thousands of years.

River Walk Image Coming Soon

River Walk

A self-guided pathway extending along the Barwon River and linking Baiame’s Ngunnhu with riverside reserves and the Ochre Beds. The walk allows visitors to experience the landscape while following local signage and respecting culturally sensitive areas.
