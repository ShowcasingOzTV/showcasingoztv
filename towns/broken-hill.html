Broken Hill (NSW)

Broken Hill is an iconic outback city in far-western New South Wales, situated close to the South Australian border on the traditional Country of the Wilyakali people. Often called the Silver City, Broken Hill was established following the discovery of an exceptionally rich silver, lead and zinc ore body during the 1880s.

The discovery transformed a remote section of the Barrier Ranges into one of Australia’s most important mining centres. It also led to the creation of the Broken Hill Proprietary Company, later known as BHP, which grew from its Broken Hill origins into one of the world’s largest resources companies.

Mining shaped the city’s physical landscape, economy and community. Headframes, mullock heaps, former mine sites and the prominent Line of Lode remain visible throughout Broken Hill, while local museums preserve mineral specimens, railway equipment and stories of those who worked underground.

Broken Hill also played a major role in the development of Australia’s labour movement. Difficult and dangerous mining conditions produced prolonged industrial disputes and helped establish workplace standards that influenced workers well beyond the city.

Beyond mining, Broken Hill has developed one of regional Australia’s most distinctive artistic communities. Its intense desert light, industrial forms and surrounding landscapes have inspired generations of artists, including Pro Hart and the Brushmen of the Bush.

Galleries, studios, museums, murals and public sculptures are spread throughout the city. Broken Hill’s architecture and outback setting have also appeared in numerous Australian films and television productions, including scenes associated with Priscilla, Queen of the Desert.

In 2015, Broken Hill became Australia’s first city to be included on the National Heritage List. Today, the city combines working mining operations with heritage streetscapes, cultural attractions, hospitality businesses and the essential services required by surrounding Far West communities.

📍 Attractions

Broken Hill offers an extensive collection of museums, galleries, tours and visitor experiences exploring mining, art, Aboriginal culture, aviation, railways and life in the Australian outback.

Explore attractions filming & feature options

  • Absalom’s Gallery A gallery presenting the work of artist Jack Absalom, whose paintings capture the colours, space and character of Australia’s outback.
  • Albert Kersten Mining and Minerals Museum A museum exploring Broken Hill’s geology, mineral wealth and mining history through local specimens, interpretive displays and the renowned Silver Tree.
  • Bell’s Milk Bar A long-running Broken Hill institution recreating the atmosphere of a traditional twentieth-century milk bar and serving classic drinks, sweets and refreshments.
  • Broken Hill City Art Gallery One of regional New South Wales’ oldest public art galleries, housed in a restored heritage building and presenting works from its collection alongside touring and contemporary exhibitions.
  • Broken Hill Cemetery Tour A guided experience sharing stories of notable residents, pioneering families, artists, workers and community figures buried in the city’s historic cemetery.
  • Broken Hill Distillery A local distillery producing spirits inspired by Broken Hill and its surrounding desert environment.
  • Broken Hill Heritage Walk Tour A guided walking tour through the city centre introducing visitors to Broken Hill’s nationally significant buildings, mining history, architecture and community stories.
  • Broken Hill Mining Museum and White’s Mineral Art Gallery A mining-themed attraction combining underground-style displays, historic equipment and distinctive mineral artworks.
  • Flying Doctor Outback Heritage Experience A visitor experience at the Royal Flying Doctor Service base explaining the organisation’s history and its continuing role in delivering healthcare across remote Australia.
  • Historical Synagogue Museum A restored former synagogue preserving the history and contribution of Broken Hill’s Jewish community.
  • Kulluwirru Dreaming Tour A guided Aboriginal cultural experience within Mutawintji National Park sharing Dreaming stories, rock art and the cultural significance of the restricted historic site.
  • McCarthurs Mineral Specimens, Crystals and Souvenirs A specialist local business displaying and selling Broken Hill mineral specimens alongside Australian and international crystals and geological pieces.
  • Mutawintji Heritage Tour An Aboriginal-guided regional experience exploring the landscape, cultural history and significant sites of Mutawintji National Park.
  • Outback Astronomy A guided stargazing experience using Broken Hill’s clear skies and low light pollution to introduce visitors to the night sky.
  • Palace Hotel An iconic Broken Hill hotel renowned for its interior murals and its connection with the film Priscilla, Queen of the Desert.
  • Pro Hart Gallery A major gallery dedicated to the work and legacy of Pro Hart, one of Australia’s best-known outback artists.
  • Quandong Farm A local farm on the Broken Hill side of the Silverton Road growing and producing products from native desert quandongs.
  • Silver City Mint and Art Centre A gallery and visitor attraction containing local artwork, jewellery, minerals and the large-scale Big Picture desert painting.
  • Sulphide Street Railway and Historical Museum A museum complex housed at the former Sulphide Street railway station, preserving locomotives, railway equipment and collections connected with Broken Hill’s social and transport history.
  • The Old Brewery A heritage attraction and local venue associated with Broken Hill’s early commercial history.
