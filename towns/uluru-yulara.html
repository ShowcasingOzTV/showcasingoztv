Uluru / Yulara (NT)

Uluru and Yulara lie in Australia’s Red Centre, approximately 450 kilometres south-west of Alice Springs. Uluru rises from the desert within Uluru-Kata Tjuta National Park, while Yulara is the nearby resort township providing accommodation, dining, tours and essential visitor services.

Uluru and Kata Tjuta form part of a living cultural landscape of profound importance to Aṉangu, the Traditional Owners. Tjukurpa, the foundation of Aṉangu law, knowledge and culture, connects the land, its features and the stories that have been passed between generations.

Uluru is internationally recognised for its immense scale, changing colour and distinctive geological form. Visitors can experience designated walks, waterholes, rock formations and viewing areas while observing cultural protocols, remaining on marked paths and respecting restricted sacred sites.

West of Uluru, Kata Tjuta comprises 36 towering domes separated by valleys and gorges. The Valley of the Winds and Walpa Gorge walks reveal another striking part of the national park, with access sometimes restricted during periods of extreme heat or for cultural and safety reasons.

Yulara was developed as a purpose-built visitor centre to reduce pressure on the national park. Today, Ayers Rock Resort brings together hotels, serviced apartments, restaurants, galleries, shops and organised cultural and desert experiences within one compact township.

📍 Attractions

Bookable cultural, dining and desert experiences around Yulara allow visitors to engage with the landscape through guided and carefully managed activities.

Explore attractions filming & feature options

  • Field of Light Uluru Bruce Munro’s large-scale light installation fills the desert with thousands of illuminated stems, creating an evening experience against the silhouettes of Uluru and the surrounding dunes.
  • Maruku Arts Cultural Experiences Aṉangu-owned cultural experiences and art activities sharing knowledge through storytelling, demonstrations and workshops led by local artists and interpreters.
  • Tali Wiru An intimate open-air dining experience on a private desert dune, combining contemporary Australian cuisine, Indigenous flavours and views towards Uluru and Kata Tjuta.
  • Uluru Camel Tours Guided camel experiences through the red desert surrounding Yulara, including sunrise, sunset and daytime rides with views across the landscape towards Uluru and Kata Tjuta.
  • Wintjiri Wiru A choreographed evening experience using drones, light, sound and projection to present an Aṉangu story in the desert near Yulara.

Kata Tjuta

A group of 36 immense rock domes west of Uluru. Kata Tjuta is deeply significant to Aṉangu and forms the second great geological landmark within the national park.

Mala Walk and Kantju Gorge

A marked route from the Mala car park past caves and important Aṉangu sites to the sheer rock walls of Kantju Gorge.

Talinguru Nyakunytjaku

A purpose-built sunrise viewing area with walking tracks and elevated platforms offering expansive views of Uluru and Kata Tjuta.
