Hillston (NSW)

Hillston is a regional town situated beside the Lachlan River in the western Riverina of New South Wales. Located at the junction of the Kidman Way and Lachlan Valley Way, it provides services and supplies for surrounding farms, agricultural businesses and smaller rural communities.

The district forms part of the traditional country of Aboriginal peoples connected with the Lachlan River and the western Riverina. Long before the development of the present town, the river and its surrounding wetlands provided water, food, movement routes and places of cultural significance.

European settlement expanded during the nineteenth century as pastoralists established sheep and cattle properties along the Lachlan River. The locality became an important river crossing for stock and travellers before developing into a permanent service centre.

Water continues to shape Hillston’s economy and landscape. Irrigation supports a diverse agricultural district producing cotton, grain, citrus, vegetables and livestock. These industries have also created employment in agricultural services, freight, processing and water management.

Hillston’s connection with the Lachlan River remains visible through riverside reserves, fishing locations, walking areas and the town’s swinging pedestrian bridge. Lake Woorabinda provides another important recreation area close to the town centre.

Beyond Hillston, the district opens into expansive pastoral country. Willandra National Park preserves the buildings and landscape of a former major sheep station, while Merriwagga reflects the history and character of the smaller communities established across the plains.

Black Stump Hotel

The Black Stump Hotel is a traditional country pub in Merriwagga, south of Hillston. Closely associated with the district’s Black Stump identity, the hotel has served residents, agricultural workers and travellers moving through the western Riverina. It remains a recognisable local meeting place and an example of the small rural pubs that continue to support isolated communities.

Willandra Homestead

Willandra Homestead stands within Willandra National Park on the site of a former major pastoral station. The present homestead was built in 1918 and formed part of a large complex supporting one of the region’s prominent Merino enterprises. Restored rooms, verandahs and surrounding station buildings provide an insight into pastoral life on the western plains.

Willandra Men’s Quarters & Cottage

The men’s quarters and cottage form part of the historic Willandra Homestead complex. These buildings help explain the different living arrangements experienced by station workers, managers and families. Together with the homestead, woolshed and other surviving structures, they reveal how a large pastoral property operated in an isolated environment.

Willandra National Park is in a remote area northwest of Hillston. Roads may be unsealed and access can be affected by wet weather. Confirm park conditions, building access and opening arrangements before travelling.

Hillston Town Centre

Hillston Town Centre contains local shops, accommodation, hospitality businesses and essential community services. High Street reflects the town’s continuing role as a commercial and service centre for surrounding farms and rural communities.

Lachlan River Swing Bridge Image Coming Soon

Lachlan River Swing Bridge

Hillston’s swinging pedestrian bridge crosses the Lachlan River and connects with riverside walking areas. The bridge provides a distinctive view of the river, its red gums and the green corridor that passes beside the town.

Lake Woorabinda Image Coming Soon

Lake Woorabinda

Lake Woorabinda is a constructed recreation lake near central Hillston. A surrounding walking track, picnic areas and open space make it a popular place for exercise, birdwatching and relaxation beside the water.

Merriwagga Image Coming Soon

Merriwagga

Merriwagga is a small agricultural village south of Hillston on the Kidman Way. The community is surrounded by broad farming country and is best known to travellers for the Black Stump Hotel and its connection with local Black Stump folklore.

River levels, rural-road conditions and access to remote locations can change following rain or flooding. Check current local and national park advice before travelling beyond Hillston.
