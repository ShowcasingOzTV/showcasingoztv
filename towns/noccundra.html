Noccundra (QLD)

Noccundra is a tiny and exceptionally remote settlement in Queensland’s Channel Country, positioned beside the Wilson River approximately 140 kilometres west of Thargomindah. Set within the immense Nockatunga pastoral property, the settlement provides travellers with an authentic experience of the isolation, landscape and resilience that characterise far south-western Queensland.

The settlement developed during the late nineteenth century near the intersection of pastoral, stock and trading routes connecting western Queensland with Innamincka and Tibooburra. A town reserve was established during the 1880s, with a hotel, store, post office and other services supporting pastoral workers and travellers moving across the region.

Noccundra never developed into a substantial township. Improvements in motor transport and the growth of larger regional centres gradually reduced the need for its store and other services. Today, the historic Noccundra Hotel is the settlement’s only surviving original occupied building and remains an important meeting place for local station families, workers and outback travellers.

Constructed from sandstone transported by camel train, the heritage hotel is recognised for its traditional outback architecture and its long association with the surrounding pastoral community. Throughout its history, it has served as a hotel, accommodation provider, traveller service point and venue for community gatherings and Royal Flying Doctor Service clinics.

The nearby Noccundra Waterhole forms part of the Wilson River and brings life to the otherwise dry Channel Country landscape. The waterhole and surrounding river environment attract birdlife and other wildlife, providing opportunities for fishing, photography and quiet observation. Seasonal flooding can dramatically transform the surrounding plains and affect access through the district.

Noccundra’s airstrip, community hall, cemetery, memorials, racecourse, rodeo ground and illuminated tennis court illustrate how a very small and isolated population maintains its community connections. Together, these places tell the story of a settlement shaped by pastoral life, extreme distances and the changing conditions of the outback.

Andrew Hume Expedition Memorial

Positioned near the Noccundra Hotel, this memorial commemorates explorer Andrew Hume’s ill-fated 1874 expedition. Hume and his party passed Noccundra Waterhole while attempting to find survivors of Ludwig Leichhardt’s missing expedition.

Image Coming Soon

Julie Kate’s Corner

Julie Kate’s Corner is an important road junction on the remote routes connecting Noccundra with Innamincka and other Channel Country destinations. The junction reflects the immense distances and limited road network encountered while travelling through this part of the outback.

Image Coming Soon

Noccundra Airstrip

The unsealed airstrip behind the Noccundra Hotel provides an important transport connection for the isolated settlement. Visiting pilots can taxi close to the hotel, making aircraft arrivals a distinctive part of Noccundra’s outback character.

Noccundra Cemetery

Noccundra’s small cemetery preserves part of the settlement’s social history and provides a quiet reminder of the residents, pastoral workers and families associated with this remote community.

Noccundra Community Hall and Tennis Court

Located near the hotel, the community hall and illuminated tennis court provide valuable recreational and gathering facilities for residents of the surrounding pastoral district. A children’s playground and public amenities are also located nearby.

Noccundra Racecourse and Rodeo Ground

These community grounds reflect the importance of campdrafting, rodeo, horse racing and other social events within isolated pastoral communities. Events bring together competitors, residents and visitors from across the wider district.

Noccundra Store Remains

The surviving wall section and foundations mark the location of Noccundra’s former general store. The store once supplied the settlement and surrounding pastoral stations before closing as transport patterns and regional services changed.

Noccundra Waterhole and Wilson River

The long waterhole beside Noccundra forms part of the Wilson River and supports birdlife and other wildlife within the dry Channel Country. Its gum-lined banks provide opportunities for fishing, birdwatching, photography and observing the changing outback environment.
