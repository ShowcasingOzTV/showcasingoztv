Borroloola (NT)

Borroloola is a remote township situated on the McArthur River in the Northern Territory’s Gulf of Carpentaria region. Located approximately 670 kilometres east-south-east of Katherine, it serves as the principal service centre for the southern Gulf and an important stopping point along the Savannah Way.

The town is home to the Yanyuwa, Garrwa, Mara and Gudanji peoples, whose continuing cultural connections to the rivers, coastline, islands and surrounding Country are central to the region’s identity. Aboriginal culture remains an important part of community life and is represented through locally produced artwork, cultural knowledge and land and sea management.

Borroloola developed as a frontier settlement during the late nineteenth century and has a distinctive history shaped by pastoral activity, transport, government administration and its remote location. The Old Borroloola Police Station Museum Heritage Precinct preserves part of this history and contains one of the Northern Territory’s oldest surviving police station properties.

Today, Borroloola provides fuel, groceries, mechanical services, health facilities, accommodation and other essential services to local residents, surrounding communities, pastoral properties and travellers crossing the Gulf region. The town can be reached from the west via the Carpentaria Highway and forms part of the Savannah Way touring route.

Fishing is one of the region’s strongest visitor attractions. The McArthur River and nearby King Ash Bay attract anglers seeking barramundi and other northern species, while the wider Gulf provides access to estuaries, wetlands, coastal waters and the Sir Edward Pellew Group of Islands.

The surrounding landscape changes dramatically between the wet and dry seasons. Travellers should check current road conditions, park access information and weather forecasts before visiting. Long distances, extreme heat and limited services also make careful vehicle preparation, adequate drinking water and responsible travel essential.

📍 Attractions

Borroloola offers opportunities to explore local Aboriginal art, regional history, Gulf Country landscapes and the fishing culture associated with the McArthur River and nearby King Ash Bay.

Explore attractions filming & feature options

  • Old Borroloola Police Station Museum Heritage Precinct This historic precinct preserves an important part of Borroloola’s frontier and administrative history. Displays, surviving buildings and local artefacts provide visitors with insight into the town’s development and the experiences of people who lived and worked in the Gulf region.
  • Waralungku Arts A community-owned Aboriginal art centre representing artists from Borroloola and the surrounding Gulf Country. Visitors can view and purchase locally produced paintings, prints, carvings, jewellery and other works while learning more about the region’s cultures and stories.

🗺️ Points of Interest & Landmarks

Explore Borroloola’s river, town centre, fishing destinations and nearby conservation reserve, which together reveal the natural and cultural character of the southern Gulf region.

Barrawulla Loop Walk Image Coming Soon

Barrawulla Loop Walk

A walking trail within Caranbirini Conservation Reserve that passes through woodland and striking sandstone formations. The surrounding landscape holds cultural significance and is associated with the Barrawulla, or white cockatoo, creation story.

Borroloola Town Centre Image Coming Soon

Borroloola Town Centre

The service centre of the southern Gulf region, providing shops, fuel, health services, community facilities and visitor accommodation. The town centre reflects Borroloola’s role as an important hub for remote communities and regional travellers.

Caranbirini Waterhole Image Coming Soon

Caranbirini Waterhole

A permanent waterhole within Caranbirini Conservation Reserve, approximately 46 kilometres south-west of Borroloola. The waterhole supports birdlife and other native wildlife and can be explored by following the reserve’s designated walking tracks.

King Ash Bay Image Coming Soon

King Ash Bay

Located approximately 50 kilometres from Borroloola on the McArthur River, King Ash Bay is one of the Gulf region’s best-known fishing destinations. Its boat access and fishing facilities attract visitors pursuing barramundi and other northern species.

Lost City Image Coming Soon

Lost City, Caranbirini Conservation Reserve

A spectacular collection of weathered sandstone pillars within Caranbirini Conservation Reserve. Some formations rise approximately 25 metres and resemble the towers and streets of an ancient city.

McArthur River Image Coming Soon

McArthur River

The major river flowing through Borroloola and towards the Gulf of Carpentaria. It supports local ecosystems, fishing activity and cultural connections while shaping the landscape and identity of the wider district.
