Daly Waters (NT)

Daly Waters is a small but historically significant outback locality in the Northern Territory, situated near the junction of the Stuart and Carpentaria highways. Located between Tennant Creek and Katherine, it has long served as an important stopping point for people travelling through Australia’s remote north.

The locality forms part of the traditional country of the Jingili people, whose cultural connections with the land, water sources and surrounding landscape extend across generations. The presence of reliable water made the area an important meeting place long before the development of the modern highway settlement.

Daly Waters became closely associated with the Overland Telegraph Line during the nineteenth century. The telegraph connected Australia with international communications networks and dramatically reduced the time required for news and information to travel between the continent and the rest of the world.

The former telegraph station and associated heritage sites recall the people who worked in extremely isolated conditions to maintain this vital communications link. The settlement’s position along the north-south corridor later reinforced its importance to travellers, transport operators and remote communities.

Daly Waters also occupies an important place in Australian aviation history. Its airfield became a refuelling and service point for early long-distance flights and was later used for military operations during the Second World War. The historic aviation complex and surviving hangar help preserve this pioneering period of Australian air travel.

Today, the Daly Waters Pub is the locality’s best-known attraction. Its historic building, outback hospitality and unusual collection of memorabilia contributed by visitors have made it one of the most recognisable pubs along the Stuart Highway.

The surrounding landscape is characterised by open savannah, native bushland, seasonal waterways and the expansive skies of the Northern Territory interior. Daly Waters provides travellers with an opportunity to pause, experience outback hospitality and explore stories connected with communications, aviation and long-distance travel.

📍 Attractions

Daly Waters offers visitors a distinctive collection of outback attractions shaped by early aviation, highway travel, local characters and the settlement’s famous hospitality.

Explore attractions filming & feature options

  • Daly Waters Aviation Complex & Flying Fox An important historic aviation precinct associated with early long-distance passenger flights, international air routes and military operations. Its surviving airfield features and hangar provide insight into the development of aviation across remote northern Australia.
  • Daly Waters Pub One of Australia’s best-known outback pubs, recognised for its historic character, traveller memorabilia, meals, entertainment and welcoming atmosphere. The pub remains a social centre and memorable stopping point for people travelling along the Stuart Highway.
  • Tim’s Junkyard An eclectic outdoor collection of machinery, vehicles, equipment and unusual objects accumulated over many years. The display reflects the resourcefulness, humour and practical character associated with life in remote Australian communities.

Daly Waters Telegraph Station

The former telegraph station site recalls Daly Waters’ role in the Overland Telegraph network, which connected Australia with overseas communications during the nineteenth century. Although little of the original station remains, the site represents an important period in the development of communication across Australia’s remote interior.

Stuart Tree

The Stuart Tree is associated with explorer John McDouall Stuart and the expeditions that helped establish an overland route through central Australia. It provides a physical connection with the era of inland exploration and the later development of the telegraph and transport corridor.
