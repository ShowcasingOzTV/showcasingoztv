Daly Waters, Northern Territory

Outback aviation history, Overland Telegraph heritage and one of Australia’s most memorable highway stopovers.

Discover Daly Waters

Daly Waters is a small but nationally recognised outback locality in the Northern Territory, situated near the junction of the Stuart and Carpentaria highways. Located between Tennant Creek and Katherine, it has long provided travellers with an important place to rest, refuel and experience the distinctive character of Australia’s remote north.

The area forms part of the traditional country of the Jingili people, whose cultural connections to the land and surrounding water sources extend across generations. The availability of reliable water made Daly Waters an important meeting place long before European exploration and later helped shape its role in communications and transport.

Daly Waters became closely connected with the development of the Overland Telegraph during the 1870s. The telegraph linked Australia with international communications networks and transformed the speed at which news could travel between the continent and the rest of the world. Remnants and stories associated with the former telegraph station remain an important part of the locality’s heritage.

The town also occupies a significant place in Australian aviation history. Daly Waters developed as an important refuelling and service point for early long-distance flights, later becoming a military airfield during the Second World War. Its historic aviation complex, hangar and airfield reflect the pioneering period when travelling across Australia by air was still a major undertaking.

Today, Daly Waters is best known by many travellers for its colourful historic pub. The building, memorabilia and contributions left by visitors from around the world have created an unusual and instantly recognisable outback atmosphere. The pub continues to serve as a social centre, accommodation provider and popular stop along the Stuart Highway journey.

Beyond its built heritage, Daly Waters is surrounded by open savannah, native bushland and the immense skies characteristic of the Northern Territory interior. Seasonal changes influence the appearance of the landscape, from dry golden grasslands to greener conditions following the northern wet season.

ShowcasingOz TV will explore the stories, businesses and historic sites that have made Daly Waters much more than a highway stop. Its enduring links with First Nations culture, telecommunications, aviation, pastoral travel and outback hospitality make it a compelling destination on the journey through Australia’s north.

Attractions In and Around Daly Waters

Daly Waters Aviation Complex & Flying Fox

Explore one of the Northern Territory’s most historically significant aviation sites. Daly Waters played an important role in early passenger flights, international air routes and military operations. The surviving hangar, airfield features, interpretive information and locally displayed Flying Fox provide insight into the challenges and achievements of pioneering outback aviation.

Daly Waters Pub

Visit one of Australia’s best-known outback pubs, established in the early twentieth century and filled with decades of traveller memorabilia. Its unusual collections, rustic buildings, meals, entertainment and welcoming outback atmosphere have made the pub a memorable stop for generations of Stuart Highway travellers.

Tim’s Junkyard

Discover an eclectic outdoor collection of machinery, vehicles, equipment and other objects accumulated over many years. Located near the Daly Waters Pub precinct, the display reflects the resourcefulness, humour and improvisational spirit often associated with life in remote Australian communities.

Daly Waters Telegraph Station

The former telegraph station site recalls Daly Waters’ role in the Overland Telegraph network, which connected Australia with overseas communications during the nineteenth century. Although little of the original station remains, the location represents an important stage in the development of communication across the continent’s remote interior.

Stuart Tree

This historic tree is associated with explorer John McDouall Stuart and the expeditions that helped establish an overland route through central Australia. The marked tree provides a physical connection to the era of European exploration and the subsequent development of the telegraph and transport corridor.
